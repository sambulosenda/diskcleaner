Reading your disk…
  1. Step 1

    Scan

    Roomy reads your disk. Scanning never changes a file, and it never follows shortcuts out of your home folder.

  2. Step 2

    Explain

    Every item gets a plain description and one of three labels: Likely safe, Review first or Keep. Your photos, documents and messages are never Likely safe.

  3. Step 3

    You choose

    Nothing is ticked for you. You tick each thing you want gone, and Roomy shows how much space that frees.

  4. Step 4

    Check again

    Just before acting, Roomy checks every item again. Anything that changed, opened or moved since the scan is skipped.

  5. Step 5

    Trash, with Put back

    Items go to the Trash, not straight off your disk. Put back returns the last clean-up to where it was.

Developer mode

Build files, simulators and caches pile up fastest on a developer's Mac. Roomy knows where each tool keeps them, which projects still use them, and the command that clears them properly.

    What Roomy will not do

    When it is unsure, Roomy leaves a file where it is.

    • No automatic clean-ups. Ever.
    • No AI agent deciding what is safe.
    • No scare warnings or alarming totals.
    • Never removes Docker volumes.
    • Never marks your photos, documents or messages Likely safe.
    • Never sends your file names anywhere.

    Questions

    Will Roomy delete something I need?

    Roomy only moves items you tick, and it moves them to the Trash so you can get them back. Your own photos, documents and messages are never labelled Likely safe, and anything in use or changed recently is skipped.

    Does Roomy send my file names anywhere?

    No. Scanning and labelling happen on your Mac. Roomy does not use an AI service to decide what is safe.

    Why does Roomy ask for Full Disk Access?

    Some of the biggest items, like iPhone backups and Mail downloads, sit in folders macOS protects. Roomy explains why before asking, and works without it too, with fewer results.

    I am not a developer. Is it for me?

    Yes. Simple mode shows everyday things like large downloads, old installers, iPhone backups and duplicates, in plain words. Developer mode is there when you want it.

    Which Macs does it run on?

    Any Mac with macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.
